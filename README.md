# ICT Development and Service Exports in Developing and Emerging Economies

## Project Overview

This project examines the relationship between Information and Communication Technology (ICT) development and service exports in developing and emerging economies.

The study uses panel data for 25 developing and emerging countries over the period 2009–2019 to investigate whether improvements in ICT development are associated with stronger service export performance.

The analysis combines panel-data econometrics with diagnostic testing to evaluate the relationship between ICT development, service exports, GDP per capita, and population.

## Research Question

**How does ICT development influence service export performance in developing and emerging economies?**

## Study Coverage

- **Countries:** 25 developing and emerging economies
- **Period:** 2009–2019
- **Observations:** Country-year panel
- **Data Sources:** World Bank and World Trade Organization (WTO)

### Countries Covered

India, Indonesia, Kazakhstan, Argentina, Bangladesh, Kenya, Malaysia, Mexico, Morocco, Brazil, Chile, China, Colombia, Egypt, Ethiopia, Ghana, Pakistan, Peru, Philippines, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Sri Lanka, Thailand, United Arab Emirates, and Vietnam.

## Variables

### Dependent Variable

**Log of Service Exports**

Service exports are used to measure a country's performance in international trade in services.

### Main Explanatory Variable

**ICT Index**

The ICT Index is constructed using:

- Mobile cellular subscriptions per 100 people
- Internet users (% of population)
- Fixed broadband subscriptions per 100 people

The indicators are normalized and combined to represent overall ICT development.

### Control Variables

- **Log GDP per capita**
- **Log Population**

## Methodology

The study applies panel-data regression techniques to account for differences across countries and changes over time.

The following approaches and diagnostic tests are used:

- Fixed Effects (FE) Model
- Random Effects (RE) Model
- Hausman Specification Test
- Correlation Analysis
- Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)
- Breusch–Pagan Test
- Autocorrelation diagnostics
- Robust Standard Errors
- Stationarity testing

The Hausman test is used to determine the preferred specification between the Fixed Effects and Random Effects models.

## Key Findings

### 1. Fixed Effects Model is Preferred

The Hausman test produces a chi-square statistic of **74.47 (p < 0.001)**, supporting the use of the Fixed Effects model over the Random Effects model.

### 2. ICT Development is Positively Associated with Service Exports

In the Fixed Effects model, the ICT Index has a coefficient of **0.4972** with a **p-value of 0.0042**, indicating a positive and statistically significant association between ICT development and log service exports.

### 3. Model Performance

The Fixed Effects model explains approximately **25.3% of the within-country variation** in log service exports.

### 4. Multicollinearity was Examined

The correlation between the ICT Index and GDP per capita is relatively high (**0.880**). Variance Inflation Factors were therefore examined, with VIF values ranging from approximately **1.22 to 4.67**.

### 5. Heteroskedasticity was Addressed

The Breusch–Pagan test indicates the presence of heteroskedasticity (**p < 0.001**). Robust standard errors were therefore used in the analysis.

## Selected Model Result

| Variable | Fixed Effects Coefficient | p-value |
|----------|---------------------------:|--------:|
| ICT Index | 0.4972 | 0.0042 |
| Log GDP per capita | -0.4574 | 0.0001 |
| Log Population | -1.1675 | 0.0001 |

## Policy Implications

The findings highlight the importance of ICT development for strengthening participation in international service trade.

The study points towards policy priorities such as:

- Expanding broadband and digital infrastructure
- Improving access to digital technologies
- Developing digital skills
- Strengthening digital governance
- Reducing barriers to trade in services
- Supporting export diversification and innovation
